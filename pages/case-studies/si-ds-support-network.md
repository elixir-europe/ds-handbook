## Why this case

When Slovenia embedded open science principles into national legislation through the Scientific Research and Innovation Activity Act (ZZrID, passed in late 2021) and the Decree on Open Science (2023), public research organizations across the country had to rapidly adapt their institutional workflows. Funded by the European Union’s Recovery and Resilience Facility through the Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Innovation (MVZI), the national SPOZNAJ project (2023–2026) — coordinated by the Central Technical Library of the University of Ljubljana (CTK) — was launched in mid-2023 to advance open science practices, build RDM capacity, standardize FAIR data workflows, and adapt local infrastructures. To meet these statutory requirements, universities and institutes designated librarians, IT staff, and researchers as institutional "data experts" or "data stewards".

However, top-down policy mandates did not automatically create operational expertise. Most of these newly designated data stewards found themselves as the sole person in their building tasked with guiding researchers through Data Management Plans (DMPs), repository selection, and FAIR data sharing — frequently without prior hands-on RDM experience. While official policy documents and webinars explained legal compliance and FAIR theory, they offered little guidance for daily stewardship friction: how to review a complex DMP draft, how to address researcher anxiety about raw data, or where to deposit niche domain datasets. Without local peers to consult, stewards faced significant operational isolation.

To bridge the gap between national mandates and practical execution, CTK established an informal peer support group (Podporna skupina podatkovnih strokovnjakov). Rather than creating another formal administrative committee, CTK set up a low-stakes, cross-institutional network anchored by monthly drop-in calls and an asynchronous Slack channel. This gave solo stewards across all 20 SPOZNAJ project partner institutions — and the broader scientific community — a direct space to unpack daily RDM challenges, exchange templates, and standardize stewardship practices together.

## What we did

The initiative began with building formal baseline capacity across the 20 consortium institutions. In autumn 2024, CTK and SPOZNAJ project partners launched the first national data stewardship training course. Modeled after established European programs — such as the Vienna University Data Steward Postgraduate Course, 4TU Essentials 4 Data Support, and EOSC Synergy — the curriculum provided an intensive 72-hour training program.

Recognizing that an intensive training course alone could not sustain stewards once they returned to their daily institutional realities, CTK established the *Podporna skupina* (support network) following this first training iteration. The initial goal was simple: set up a recurring 1-hour Zoom call on the first Friday of every month, backed by a dedicated Slack workspace, to prevent newly trained stewards from drifting back into isolation.

In the beginning, monthly meetings leaned heavily on formal presentations about legislation, national infrastructure, and overarching policy framework details. However, as trust grew, the structure naturally shifted from top-down lectures to bottom-up peer exchange. Stewards began presenting their own institutional setups, walking colleagues through local workflows, and openly discussing friction points. The Slack workspace became the primary asynchronous desk for quick sanity checks, sharing event announcements, and discussing edge cases. This peer-led momentum directly informed the second iteration of the data steward course in autumn-winter 2025/2026, which onboarded a new cohort of stewards into active support network.

As community members gained confidence, participants began contributing back to the network. For instance, building on experience from the front lines, community members helped deliver shorter online case studies for broader research audiences and authored detailed, institution- and domain-specific guidance for filling out the national mandatory Data Management Plan (DMP) templates.

## What changed

What started as a top-down mandate transformed into an active, self-sustaining community of practice across Slovenian research institutions. Rather than struggling in isolation, data stewards now have a direct line to peers across 20 public research organizations and beyond.

Key outputs and outcomes include:

* **Structured Training Pipeline:** Delivered two comprehensive data stewardship courses (Autumn 2024 and Autumn–Winter 2025/2026), creating a shared baseline of expertise across the country, with all training materials and lecture recordings openly published and made publicly available.
* **Practical Stewardship Guidance:** Produced localized, domain-specific guidance and practical advice for navigating the mandatory national NRRP DMP templates.
* **Continuous Async Problem-Solving:** Established an active Slack network that serves as an informal helpdesk where stewards trade repository recommendations, share workshop announcements, and troubleshoot daily RDM hurdles. Recognizing that RDM needs extend beyond consortium boundaries, CTK opened membership to anyone interested in data stewardship across the country, regardless of whether their institution was an official project partner (`#podporna-skupina-external`). All monthly discussions, Slack exchanges, and informal materials are conducted in Slovenian.
* **Institutional Benchmarking:** Created a safe space where solo stewards can compare institutional approaches, giving them the evidence and confidence needed to advocate for better RDM workflows with local management.
* **Direct Access to Domain Experts:** The regular calls and Slack channel broke down institutional silos, helping stewards establish personal acquaintances and direct contact lines for advice with domain specialists across different research disciplines.

## What we'd tell others

* **What worked, and why.** Combining formal, high-intensity training with an informal, low-barrier peer support group. The initial 19-day training was essential not only for establishing a shared vocabulary, but for forging the initial personal connections and mutual trust among participants. The monthly drop-in calls and Slack workspace then provided long-term sustainability. Pushing the monthly meetings to move past policy lectures into honest showcases of institutional struggles was the single best pivot for member engagement.
* **What didn't work, and how we adapted.** Early meetings were too formal and agenda-heavy, causing participants to act as passive listeners. The group adapted by encouraging members to bring real-world problems and work-in-progress materials. Allowing open, candid discussions about what *didn't* work at a specific institute built far more trust than polished success stories.
* **Transferability and scaling potential.** This model is highly transferable to other national or regional contexts, provided organizers distinguish between platform setup and community building. While setting up digital channels like Slack and Zoom takes minimal administrative effort, forging active cross-institutional relationships requires the leverage of a funded project framework (such as SPOZNAJ). Dedicated project funding is critical to formalize data steward roles across organizations, protect staff hours for peer participation, and deliver the intensive baseline training that builds trust and capacity. Once that funded foundation brings people into the room, the ongoing peer support network can easily sustain itself on low operational costs.
* **Sustainability and next steps.** Although the initial SPOZNAJ project formally concluded in mid-2026, continuity is secured through an immediate follow-up funding phase running through 2030, specifically dedicated to maintaining data steward positions and sustaining the established framework. It also ensures that CTK-hosted monthly meetings, the Slack workspace, and the ongoing rollout of SPOZNAJ outcomes continue without interruption. Ultimately, this extended funding aims to embed data stewardship into daily research operations so that established steward roles and the network persist independently, even after dedicated project funding ends.
